# Card Dispute and Chargeback Procedure

**Document ID:** BM-CRD-003 | **Version:** 1.0 | **Effective:** 2026-01-01 | **Owner:** Card Disputes

*SYNTHETIC TRAINING DOCUMENT - for educational simulation only.*

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This procedure explains how branch employees receive, register and follow up a customer dispute about a card transaction. A dispute is a formal objection by a cardholder to a transaction that appears on a debit card account or a credit card statement. The procedure covers which disputes are eligible, the filing window, the information that must be collected, provisional credit, the investigation timeline, the possible outcomes and how the customer is kept informed. It applies to Classic Debit, Platinum Debit, Standard Credit and Gold Credit cards. It does not apply to disagreements about fees or interest, which are handled under the complaints process, and it does not replace the Lost and Stolen Card Procedure (BM-CRD-002), which must be followed first if the card is missing.

## 2. Key Terms

### 2.1 Dispute and Chargeback

A dispute is the request received from the customer. A chargeback is the formal claim that the bank sends through the card network to the merchant's bank to recover the amount of a disputed transaction. The employee registers the dispute. The Card Disputes team decides whether a chargeback is raised.

### 2.2 Provisional Credit

Provisional credit is a temporary credit of the disputed amount to the customer's account or credit card balance while the investigation continues. It is not a final decision. The credit can be reversed if the investigation finds that the dispute is not valid.

## 3. Eligible and Ineligible Disputes

### 3.1 Eligible Disputes

The employee may register a dispute in the following cases. The customer did not authorise the transaction and the card was not in the customer's control, for example a card-not-present fraud. The customer was charged twice for the same purchase. The amount charged is different from the amount on the receipt. The customer paid for goods or services that were never delivered or provided. The customer returned the goods or cancelled the service and the merchant did not refund the money in the agreed period. An ATM withdrawal was debited but the cash was not dispensed, or was only partly dispensed. A recurring payment continued after the customer cancelled it in writing with the merchant.

### 3.2 Ineligible Disputes

The employee must not register a dispute in the following cases and must explain the reason to the customer. The customer made the transaction and simply regrets the purchase. The transaction was completed with the chip and the correct PIN, and the customer shared the PIN with another person. The transaction is a fee, tax or interest charged by the bank itself. The dispute is about the quality of goods when the customer has no evidence of contacting the merchant. The transaction is older than the filing window in section 4. The transaction was made by a person the customer authorised to use the card, such as a family member.

### 3.3 Exceptions

If a customer insists on a dispute that looks ineligible, the employee does not refuse to take the written request. The employee registers the request with the note "customer insists" and the supervisor decides. A late dispute, which means one filed after the 60 day window, may still be accepted by the supervisor only if the customer gives written evidence that the delay was caused by a hospital stay or travel outside the country, supported by a document. The Card Disputes team has the final decision.

## 4. Filing Window

The customer must file the dispute within 60 days of the statement date on which the transaction appears. For a debit card the statement date is the date of the account statement that includes the transaction. For a credit card it is the statement date of the monthly credit card statement. The employee must check the date at the start of the interview. If the 60 days have passed, the employee explains that the dispute is outside the window and follows the exception rule in section 3.3. The employee must not change the transaction date to fit the window.

## 5. Procedure for Registering a Dispute

1. The employee verifies the customer's identity with the original valid Egyptian national ID. A person who is not the cardholder cannot file a dispute.
2. The employee asks the customer to describe the problem and decides whether it is an eligible dispute using section 3.
3. The employee checks the filing window as described in section 4.
4. The employee checks whether the card is still in the customer's hand. If the card is lost or stolen, or if the customer suspects that the card details were stolen, the employee first blocks the card as set out in the Lost and Stolen Card Procedure (BM-CRD-002).
5. The employee collects the information listed in section 6.
6. The employee registers the dispute in the card system under the CARD_SERVICE category, attaches the evidence and gives the customer the dispute reference number.
7. The employee explains the timeline in section 7 and tells the customer how the customer will be informed of the result.

## 6. Information to Collect

For every dispute the employee must record the following. The cardholder's name and the last 4 digits of the card number. The transaction date, the amount in EGP or the foreign currency, and the merchant name as shown on the statement. The reason for the dispute from the list of eligible reasons. The date when the customer first noticed the transaction. A short statement of what happened, in the customer's own words. The customer's signature on the dispute form.

Depending on the reason, the employee must also collect supporting documents. For goods not received, the order confirmation and any messages with the merchant. For a duplicate charge, both receipts or both lines of the statement. For a wrong amount, the receipt or invoice. For a cancelled service or recurring payment, the written cancellation and the date it was sent. For an ATM cash problem, the ATM receipt if available, and the date, time and location of the ATM. For an unauthorised transaction, a statement from the customer that the card was in the customer's possession and that the PIN was not shared. The employee must not wait for every document before registering the dispute, but must tell the customer which documents are still missing and that they should be provided within 10 working days.

## 7. Provisional Credit and Investigation Timeline

### 7.1 Provisional Credit

The bank gives provisional credit within 10 working days of the date the dispute is registered. The employee does not apply the credit manually. The Card Disputes team applies it in the card system and the customer sees it as a credit line. Provisional credit is not given in these cases: the dispute is for an amount below EGP 100, the dispute is registered with the note "customer insists", or the required evidence was not provided within 10 working days of registration. For the third case the credit is given as soon as the evidence is received.

### 7.2 Investigation Timeline

The investigation is completed within 45 days of the date the dispute is registered. During the investigation the Card Disputes team reviews the evidence, contacts the merchant's bank and decides whether to raise a chargeback. The 45 day period can be extended only if the card network has not responded, and the customer must be told about the extension in writing before the 45 days end. The employee must not promise the customer a result before the investigation has finished.

## 8. Outcomes

### 8.1 Dispute Accepted

If the dispute is accepted, the provisional credit becomes final. The customer is informed by SMS and, if the customer asks, by a letter at the branch. No fee is charged for an accepted dispute.

### 8.2 Dispute Rejected

If the dispute is rejected, the provisional credit is reversed and the transaction stays on the account. The customer is told the reason for the rejection. If the credit reversal makes the account balance negative or pushes the credit card over its limit, the over-limit fee of EGP 100 on credit cards may apply, and the customer should be informed of this at the time the rejection is communicated. A supervisor may waive up to EGP 200 per request if the customer asks, as fee waivers need supervisor approval.

### 8.3 Partial Acceptance

A dispute may be accepted for only a part of the amount, for example when the merchant shows that a part of the order was delivered. In that case only the accepted part becomes final, and the rest of the provisional credit is reversed.

### 8.4 Customer Disagreement

If the customer disagrees with the outcome, the customer can submit a complaint. The employee acknowledges the complaint within 2 working days and it should be resolved within 15 working days. If it is still unresolved it is escalated to the Branch Manager, and after 30 days to the banking regulator's complaints unit.

## 9. Customer Communication

The employee must keep the customer informed at each stage. At registration the customer gets the reference number and the timeline. When provisional credit is applied the customer is told by SMS. If more documents are needed the employee contacts the customer by phone using only the mobile number registered on the profile, and confirms in writing. When the investigation ends the customer is told the outcome and the reason. The employee must be honest about timelines and must not guarantee the result. The employee must never ask the customer for the PIN, the OTP or the full card number. If the customer calls to check the status, the employee confirms identity using the registered mobile number and national ID before giving any detail.

## 10. Special Cases

### 10.1 Foreign Currency Transactions

For a transaction made in a foreign currency, the employee records the original amount and currency as well as the EGP amount on the statement. Exchange rate differences between the transaction date and the posting date are not a valid reason for a dispute.

### 10.2 Repeat Disputes

If a customer files more than 3 disputes in a period of 6 months, the employee informs the supervisor, who may ask the Card Disputes team to review the card for compromise and may recommend a card replacement under the Lost and Stolen Card Procedure (BM-CRD-002).
